Floor soundproofing is a soundproofing countermeasure where "who needs it and who doesn't" is hard to understand. Unlike walls and windows, "sound transmitted from floors" is hard to imagine, and without knowing your own case, you'll waste money on unnecessary countermeasures.

This article provides <strong>a diagnostic guide to quickly determine whether floor soundproofing is needed or not</strong>. Structured to start with diagnosis, then reasons by persona, then move to realistic countermeasures, guiding optimal decisions by situation for second floor, rental, instruments, and child-rearing. Understanding the difference between vibration sounds and airborne sounds, this content enables only those who need it to practice effective countermeasures.

## Introduction: Why It's Hard to Understand "Who Needs and Who Doesn't Need Floor Soundproofing"

### Unlike Walls and Windows, "Sound Transmitted from Floors" is Hard to Imagine

Floor soundproofing differs from wall and window soundproofing in that <strong>"sound transmitted from floors" is hard to imagine</strong>.

<strong>Wall and Window Soundproofing</strong>
- <strong>Easy to understand with the sense of sound being "heard"</strong>
- <strong>Easy to feel countermeasure effects</strong>

<strong>Floor Soundproofing</strong>
- <strong>Hard to understand because sound is transmitted "as vibration"</strong>
- <strong>Hard to feel countermeasure effects</strong>

### Actually Complaints Occur from "Vibration and Impact Sounds"

Floor soundproofing becomes necessary due to <strong>vibration and impact sounds</strong>.

<strong>Characteristics of Vibration Sounds</strong>
- <strong>Transmitted through floor to lower floor</strong>
- <strong>Low-frequency sounds transmit easily</strong>
- <strong>Footsteps, dropping objects, instrument keystrokes, etc.</strong>

<strong>Characteristics of Impact Sounds</strong>
- <strong>Sounds that directly impact floor</strong>
- <strong>Thud, bang sounds</strong>
- <strong>Children running around, jumping, etc.</strong>

### Without Knowing Your Own Case, You Waste Money on Unnecessary Countermeasures

Without knowing your own case, you'll waste money on unnecessary countermeasures like:

<strong>Common Mistakes</strong>
- <strong>Investing in floor soundproofing when voices and daily life sounds are main</strong>
- <strong>Doing floor soundproofing countermeasures when living on first floor</strong>
- <strong>Prioritizing floor soundproofing when game streaming is main</strong>

<strong>Correct Understanding</strong>
- <strong>Floor soundproofing is effective for "vibration sounds"</strong>
- <strong>Voices and high frequencies leak from walls and doors, not floors</strong>
- <strong>Countermeasures matching your own case are needed</strong>

### Conclusion: "Situation Diagnosis" is Essential First

To determine whether floor soundproofing is needed or not, <strong>"situation diagnosis" is essential first</strong>.

<strong>Diagnosis Flow</strong>
1. Check your own situation
2. Determine those who need it and those who don't
3. If needed, select optimal countermeasures

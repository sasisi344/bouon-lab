"I want to start streaming or producing music seriously in my new home this spring. But soundproof rooms are expensive..."
"It's just an extension of my hobby, but I am making some revenue. Can I write this off as a business expense?"

When setting up a soundproof environment to coincide with the start of a new life in spring, the most important thing to focus on is <strong>"Substantial cost reduction through tax saving measures."</strong> A soundproof room is never a cheap purchase, but if you follow the correct procedures, you can record a certain percentage of the purchase cost as an "expense," drastically reducing the taxes you have to pay (essentially buying it at a discount).

In this article, we'll explain the roadmap to effectively turning your hobby into a "business" and wisely expensing your soundproof room.

## 1. The Magic of the "Notification of Opening of Business"

The absolute condition for expensing a soundproof room is <strong>"Using it for business (work)."</strong> A hobby karaoke room or a movie-watching room cannot be expensed for a single yen.

However, if you are starting to stream on YouTube, sell music on platforms like Audiostock, or take narration orders on freelance sites, it's a different story.

### First, Become a "Sole Proprietor"

At the timing of starting your new life in spring, submit a <strong>"Notification of Opening of Business" (開業届 - Kaigyo Todoke)</strong> and an <strong>"Application for Approval of Blue Return" (青色申告承認申請書 - Aoiro Shinkoku Shonin Shinseisho)</strong> to the tax office. These can be submitted for free.
By doing this, your streaming or production activities will be officially recognized as a "business," granting you the powerful right to expense the "soundproof room" that is essential for that business.

## 2. How Much Can You Actually Save? An Expense Simulation

Why does expensing a soundproof room result in an "effective discount"? It's because <strong>it lowers your income tax and resident tax.</strong>

### Rough Calculation: Effectively 20% to 30% OFF

Suppose your combined income tax and resident tax rate for your main job (or side job) is 20%.
If you purchase a 1.5 million yen unit-type soundproof room (like Yamaha Avitecs) for business and can expense the full amount:

`1.5 million yen × 20% = 300,000 yen`

In short, <strong>the taxes you were planning to pay will be reduced by 300,000 yen,</strong> meaning you effectively bought the soundproof room for 1.2 million yen. This is the trick behind "Expensing = Effective Discount."

## 3. The Soundproof Room Traps: "Depreciation" and "Household Apportionment"

However, you cannot expense the entire 1.5 million yen in a lump sum in the "year you bought it" (with some special exceptions). Here, two major walls stand in your way.

### Wall 1: Depreciation (Expensing over 15 years)

Unlike a PC (4 years), a soundproof room is considered a "long-lasting asset." For a unit-type (assembly) soundproof room, the legal useful life is typically <strong>"15 years (Equipment and Fixtures)."</strong>

In other words, the basic rule is to <strong>"Expense the 1.5 million yen soundproof room in increments of 100,000 yen every year, over 15 years."</strong>

### Wall 2: Household Apportionment (Excluding Private Use)

This is the point most scrutinized during tax audits.
If asked, "You also use this soundproof room at night for your hobby of gaming or watching movies, right?", it won't be recognized as 100% for business use.

- <strong>Solution:</strong> Divide it by usage time or area ratio (Household Apportionment).
  Honest declaration is required, such as, "Of a day, 70% is used for business (streaming/production), 30% is a hobby," keeping rational explanatory records (streaming logs or work journals), and keeping the expense claim to 70% (70,000 yen annually).

## 4. New vs. Used: Which is Better for Tax Savings?

Actually, from a tax-saving perspective, <strong>"Used soundproof rooms"</strong> have a major advantage.
This is because if you purchase a used asset that has passed its legal useful life (15 years), there is a calculation rule (simplified method) for shortening the useful life.

- <strong>New unit soundproof room:</strong> Expensed over 15 years
- <strong>15-year-old used soundproof room:</strong> Can potentially be expensed in as short as <strong>"3 years" (because the elapsed years are shortened).</strong>

For a sole proprietor wanting to "Expense it off quickly (compress profits in a hurry)," a used soundproof room can be an extremely powerful tax-saving tool.

## 5. Creating "Evidence" to Avoid Failure in Your New Life

To ensure your claim is not denied (not recognized as an expense) during your tax return, make sure to keep the following evidence:

1. <strong>Receipts, Estimates, and Delivery Details</strong>: Not just the soundproof room itself, but transportation and installation costs are also included in the acquisition cost.
2. <strong>Records of Business Use</strong>: YouTube streaming history, dates and times of delivered audio data, etc.
3. <strong>Photos</strong>: Take photos to show that only "business equipment (microphone, PC, lighting, etc.)" is installed inside the soundproof room, proving it is not a private space.

## Conclusion: In Your New Life, Pair "Opening a Business" with Your Soundproof Room!

If you can turn the expensive investment of a soundproof room from mere "consumption" into "business investment," your subsequent tax burden will be significantly reduced.

Spring offers a new life. If you're introducing a soundproof room to a new house or room, why not take the plunge and step up your activities as a "business"?

_Note: This article explains general tax concepts. Individual tax judgments and the "application of useful life (whether it's considered a building attachment, etc.)" vary greatly depending on the situation. Before making an expensive purchase, be sure to confirm with a tax accountant or your local tax office._

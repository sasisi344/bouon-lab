Choosing streaming microphones, wondering "dynamic or condenser?"?

Actually, <strong>your optimal microphone depends on your soundproofing environment</strong>. Expensive condenser mics can paradoxically worsen audio quality with inadequate soundproofing.

This article thoroughly explains microphone selection by soundproofing level with respective advantages and disadvantages.

## Basic Differences: Dynamic vs. Condenser

### Dynamic Microphones

<strong>Mechanism: Physical vibration captures sound</strong>
- <strong>Durable and robust</strong>
- <strong>Lower sensitivity = picks up close sounds only</strong>
- <strong>No power required (XLR connection)</strong>
- <strong>Type used in live performances and karaoke</strong>

<strong>Representative Models:</strong>
- <strong>Shure SM58 (classic, ~10,000 yen)</strong>
- <strong>Shure SM7B (streamer favorite, ~50,000 yen)</strong>
- <strong>Audio-Technica AT2040 (value, ~15,000 yen)</strong>

### Condenser Microphones

<strong>Mechanism: Electrically amplifies sound</strong>
- <strong>Delicate, high quality</strong>
- <strong>High sensitivity = picks up distant sounds too</strong>
- <strong>Power required (phantom power or USB)</strong>
- <strong>Type used in professional studios</strong>

<strong>Representative Models:</strong>
- <strong>Audio-Technica AT2020 (entry, ~10,000 yen)</strong>
- <strong>Blue Yeti (USB popular, ~15,000 yen)</strong>
- <strong>Rode NT1-A (professional, ~25,000 yen)</strong>

## Microphone Selection by Soundproofing Level

### Minimal Soundproofing (D-40 or Less)

<strong>Environment:</strong>
- <strong>Regular room</strong>
- <strong>Slight acoustic treatment</strong>
- <strong>External noise audible</strong>
- <strong>Ambient sounds present</strong>

<strong>Recommendation: Dynamic Microphone</strong>

Reasoning:
- <strong>Lower sensitivity ignores distant ambient noise</strong>
- <strong>Focuses on voice directly in front</strong>
- <strong>Forgiving of imperfect environments</strong>
- <strong>Better signal-to-noise ratio</strong>

<strong>Optimal Models:</strong>
- <strong>Shure SM58:</strong> All-around excellent
- <strong>ATR2100x:</strong> USB/XLR dual, great value
- <strong>Samson Q2U:</strong> Budget-friendly alternative

### Moderate Soundproofing (D-45~55)

<strong>Environment:</strong>
- <strong>Vocal booth or well-treated room</strong>
- <strong>External noise significantly reduced</strong>
- <strong>Controlled ambient sounds</strong>
- <strong>Occasional faint intrusions</strong>

<strong>Recommendation: Dynamic OR Entry Condenser</strong>

Dynamic Advantages:
- <strong>Still forgiving of occasional noise</strong>
- <strong>More consistent results</strong>
- <strong>Less temperamental</strong>

<strong>Condenser Benefits:</strong>
- <strong>Noticeably better quality</strong>
- <strong>Captures vocal nuance</strong>
- <strong>More professional sound</strong>

<strong>Decision Point:</strong> Test both if possible, choose based on actual results.

<strong>Optimal Models:</strong>
- <strong>Dynamic:</strong> Shure SM7B, Electro-Voice RE20
- <strong>Condenser:</strong> AT2020, Rode PodMic (dynamic character)

### Excellent Soundproofing (D-55~65)

<strong>Environment:</strong>
- <strong>Quality soundproof room</strong>
- <strong>External noise minimal</strong>
- <strong>Controlled acoustics</strong>
- <strong>Quiet baseline</strong>

<strong>Recommendation: Condenser Microphone</strong>

Reasoning:
- <strong>Soundproofing adequate for sensitivity</strong>
- <strong>Quality advantages fully realized</strong>
- <strong>Professional results achievable</strong>
- <strong>Captures vocal detail beautifully</strong>

<strong>Optimal Models:</strong>
- <strong>Rode NT1-A:</strong> Excellent value
- <strong>AKG C214:</strong> Professional grade
- <strong>Neumann TLM 102:</strong> Premium choice

### Professional Studio (D-65+)

<strong>Environment:</strong>
- <strong>Professional soundproof room</strong>
- <strong>Near-silent baseline</strong>
- <strong>Optimized acoustics</strong>
- <strong>Controlled environment</strong>

<strong>Recommendation: Premium Condenser</strong>

Reasoning:
- <strong>Environment supports highest quality</strong>
- <strong>Investment justified by results</strong>
- <strong>Professional standards achievable</strong>

<strong>Optimal Models:</strong>
- <strong>Neumann U87:</strong> Industry standard (~300,000 yen)
- <strong>AKG C414:</strong> Versatile professional (~100,000 yen)
- <strong>Rode NT2-A:</strong> Excellent value (~40,000 yen)

## Common Microphone Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Expensive Mic in Noisy Room

<strong>Problem:</strong> Condenser picks up all ambient noise
<strong>Result:</strong> Worse quality than cheap dynamic
<strong>Solution:</strong> Match mic to environment, not budget

### Mistake 2: Wrong Distance

<strong>Problem:</strong> Too close = plosives, too far = ambient noise
<strong>Solution:</strong> Optimal distance varies by mic type (see guide above)

### Mistake 3: No Pop Filter

<strong>Problem:</strong> P, B, T sounds create pops
<strong>Solution:</strong> $10 pop filter solves this instantly

### Mistake 4: Ignoring Room Acoustics

<strong>Problem:</strong> Great mic in echoey room = poor results
<strong>Solution:</strong> Treat room acoustics before upgrading microphones

## Testing and Verification

### A/B Comparison

<strong>Test Protocol:</strong>
1. Record sample with current setup
2. Make one change
3. Record identical sample
4. Compare directly
5. Keep improvement, revert if worse

<strong>Changes to Test:</strong>
- <strong>Different mic types</strong>
- <strong>Positioning variations</strong>
- <strong>Acoustic treatment additions</strong>
- <strong>Software filter adjustments</strong>

### Viewer Feedback

<strong>Valuable Metrics:</strong>
- <strong>Audio quality comments</strong>
- <strong>Retention during talking segments</strong>
- <strong>Comparison to other streamers</strong>
- <strong>Direct requests for improvements</strong>

## Conclusion

Streaming microphone selection requires matching equipment to environment:

1. <strong>Assess your soundproofing first</strong>
2. <strong>Choose mic type accordingly (dynamic for noisy, condenser for quiet)</strong>
3. <strong>Invest in acoustic treatment alongside mic</strong>
4. <strong>Use proper technique and positioning</strong>
5. <strong>Test thoroughly before committing</strong>

### Quick Decision Guide

<strong>Your Room:</strong>
- <strong>Noisy/Untreated → Dynamic mic (SM58, ATR2100x)</strong>
- <strong>Moderately treated → Dynamic or entry condenser</strong>
- <strong>Well soundproofed → Quality condenser (NT1-A, C214)</strong>
- <strong>Professional studio → Premium condenser (U87, C414)</strong>

Great audio results from environment + equipment + technique combined. Don't expect microphones alone to overcome poor room acoustics. Invest holistically for optimal streaming audio quality.

Remember: "Good enough" audio quality is achieved surprisingly affordably with smart room treatment and technique. Save money for other content improvements rather than over-investing in microphones beyond environment capabilities.

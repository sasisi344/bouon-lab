Are you wondering if you can DIY a Japanese closet (oshiire) into a soundproof room?

When recording at night, you hear "insects, wind, and car sounds." Your ASMR listeners point out background noise. You're renting, so professional soundproofing isn't possible, but you want to do something... With limited space in your room, the idea of "can I record in the closet?" naturally comes to mind.

To give you the answer upfront: <strong>While turning a closet into a fully soundproof room isn't realistic, using it as an "acoustic booth" optimized for ASMR recording is one of the most cost-effective options available in Japan.</strong>

This article provides practical information on the possibilities and precautions of DIYing a closet into a soundproof room, specific construction methods, differences from professional soundproof rooms, and smarter alternatives.

## Why More People Want to Use Closets as Recording Booths

There are several reasons behind the growing demand to use Japanese closets (oshiire) as recording booths.

### Closets Still Exist in Many Modern Japanese Homes

Closets remain a common storage form in renovated traditional houses and homes with Japanese-style rooms (washitsu). They're also still found in many rental properties, from studio apartments to regional rentals.

Especially in rental studio apartments, closets are often left unused as "unused space," making it natural to want to utilize them effectively.

### Closets Make Sense as Small Booths

Using a closet as a recording booth actually has logical advantages.

<strong>Small spaces are advantageous for recording</strong>

Small spaces reduce sound reflections, which is beneficial for recording environments. For purposes like ASMR recording that capture subtle sounds, suppressing indoor reflections is crucial.

<strong>Visual barriers help concentration</strong>

Entering the closet blocks visual stimuli, creating an environment conducive to recording focus. This is an important factor for streaming and recording work.

<strong>Low cost way to create a "private room" feeling</strong>

Since closets utilize existing structures, you can create a recording space with a "private room" feeling at a much lower cost than purchasing a new soundproof room (800,000 yen / approximately $5,300 USD or more).

## The Reality of DIY Closet Soundproofing (Important Challenges to Know)

Using a closet as a soundproof room comes with several practical challenges. Understanding these before choosing the right application is important.

### Why Closets Aren't Ideal for Soundproofing

Achieving complete soundproofing in a closet faces the following structural challenges:

<strong>Two-tier structure with thin walls and floors makes sound leak easily</strong>

Closets typically have a two-tier structure with thin walls and floors. This makes sound leak easily, and achieving complete sound isolation is difficult. Particularly, low-frequency sound isolation is challenging due to structural constraints.

<strong>Virtually no ventilation → heat builds up</strong>

Closets have almost no ventilation openings, so using them as sealed spaces causes heat to build up easily. For extended recording sessions, this can lead to stuffiness and equipment failure risks.

<strong>Adding soundproofing materials (gypsum boards, etc.) creates excessive weight</strong>

Achieving professional-level soundproofing requires heavy materials like gypsum boards and soundproofing sheets. However, closet structures aren't designed to bear such heavy loads, creating risks of excessive structural stress.

### However, It's Most Cost-Effective for "ASMR Recording"

While turning a closet into a fully soundproof room is difficult, <strong>for ASMR recording purposes, it's one of the most cost-effective options.</strong>

<strong>Whispering benefits more from "absorption than isolation"</strong>

For ASMR recording, suppressing indoor reflections through "absorption" is more important than completely blocking external sounds. When recording subtle sounds like whispers, indoor reflections significantly affect sound quality, so applying acoustic treatment in a small space can achieve sufficient quality.

<strong>Indoor reflections are a bigger enemy than external noise</strong>

In ASMR recording, indoor reflections often affect sound quality more than external environmental noise. Small spaces like closets have the advantage of being easier to suppress reflections.

<strong>Creating "air that doesn't pick up noise" is more practical than perfect isolation</strong>

Rather than achieving complete sound isolation, minimizing noise within the recording environment—creating "air that doesn't pick up noise"—is more practical for ASMR recording. Applying acoustic treatment to a closet can create this "air that doesn't pick up noise."

## Specific Construction Method: Steps to Turn a Closet into an ASMR Mini Recording Studio

Here's how to turn a closet into a mini recording studio optimized for ASMR recording. This method focuses on "absorption rather than isolation" for safety and affordability.

### ① Always Use the Space as "One Box"

When using a closet as a recording booth, it's important to <strong>use the space as one box</strong> without removing the upper and lower tier dividers.

Don't force demolition—simply "creating an acoustic space inside" is sufficient. Breaking the closet structure can cause restoration issues in rental properties, so utilizing the existing structure is safer and more practical.

### ② Cover Reflective Surfaces on Walls, Ceiling, and Door with Acoustic Panels

Covering reflective surfaces inside the closet with acoustic panels can significantly reduce indoor reflections.

<strong>Recommended Configuration</strong>

- <strong>Apply acoustic material (3-5cm thick) to wall surfaces</strong>: Covering all wall surfaces with acoustic material suppresses sound reflections. Use commercial acoustic panels or urethane foam.
- <strong>Cover door back with felt or soundproof curtains</strong>: Applying felt or soundproof curtains to the inside of the door suppresses sound reflections from the door.
- <strong>Suppress floor reflections with rugs or EVA mats</strong>: Placing rugs or EVA mats on the floor suppresses sound reflections from the floor.

This configuration can reduce indoor reflections by 80-90%, achieving sufficient sound quality for whisper and close-mic recording.

### ③ Minimal Configuration for Budget-Conscious Users

For those wanting to keep costs down, here's a minimal configuration:

- <strong>100-yen store felt × several sheets</strong>: Used as basic acoustic material for closet walls
- <strong>Commercial acoustic panels × 6-12 sheets</strong>: For more effective acoustic treatment
- <strong>USB fan (heat countermeasure)</strong>: Used for ventilation

With this configuration, you can turn a closet into an ASMR recording mini studio for <strong>approximately 9,000-15,000 yen total (approximately $60-100 USD)</strong>.

### ④ Microphone Position Dramatically Changes Sound Quality

Microphone position inside the closet significantly affects sound quality.

<strong>Keep binaural/condenser mics away from walls</strong>

Getting too close to walls makes reflected sound from walls more likely to enter the microphone. Keep the microphone 20-30cm away from walls.

<strong>Create a whisper-specific position 10-15cm from mouth</strong>

For ASMR recording, the distance from mouth to microphone is important. Creating a whisper-specific position at about 10-15cm achieves optimal sound quality.

## Dangers of Trying to Make a Closet into a Professional Soundproof Room

Attempting to turn a closet into a fully soundproof room can cause structural problems and safety issues.

### Weight Problem (Soundproofing Materials Are Heavy)

Achieving professional-level sound isolation requires heavy materials like gypsum boards, soundproofing sheets, and double walls. However, closet structures aren't designed to bear such heavy loads, creating risks of excessive structural stress.

Especially in rental properties, damaging the structure can result in high restoration costs.

### Ventilation Problem

Attempting to achieve sound isolation by completely sealing the closet causes ventilation problems.

<strong>Stuffy within minutes in sealed space</strong>

Since closets originally have almost no ventilation openings, completely sealing them makes the space stuffy within minutes. This isn't suitable for extended recording sessions.

<strong>Heat buildup can cause ASMR equipment failure</strong>

Without ventilation, heat from equipment builds up, increasing the risk of failure for microphones and audio interfaces used in ASMR recording.

👉 <strong>Conclusion: Using a closet as an "acoustic booth" rather than a "soundproof room" is optimal. ASMR is most compatible with these conditions.</strong>

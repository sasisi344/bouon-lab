Finding a rental in Sendai where you can practice freely is challenging. Soundproof or “music-friendly” listings are limited, and the most attractive rooms disappear within days. This guide summarizes Japan-specific data to help music students and performers act quickly.

## Market Snapshot in Sendai

- Approximately 320 soundproof-capable rentals exist citywide (September 2025, in-house research). About 90 include dedicated soundproof rooms; the remainder are “instrument-friendly”.
- Soundproof-room units typically cost <strong>¥70,000–¥100,000 per month</strong>. Newly built listings hover around ¥120,000.
- Rough breakdown of tenants: 60% music students/teachers, 20% streamers/creators, 20% working professionals.

## Contract Checklist

1. <strong>Practice Hours in Writing</strong>: Ensure the lease or addendum lists permitted hours and instruments. Avoid verbal-only agreements.
2. <strong>Restoration Terms</strong>: If you plan to add removable acoustic panels, confirm the restoration policy with both landlord and contractor.
3. <strong>Moving Logistics</strong>: Upright pianos need 90 cm-wide hallways and elevators. Notify management in advance; some buildings require service entrance usage.
4. <strong>Noise Measurement</strong>: Bring a portable SPL meter to viewings—test daytime and nighttime noise, including rainy-day road sounds.

## Takeaway: Secure Your Sound-Friendly Base

- Expect ¥70k–¥100k rents; share your wish list with brokers before the busy season.
- Understand area characteristics—Aoba for access, Taihaku for price-performance, Izumi for family or teaching setups.
- Verify contract clauses, logistics, and insurance upfront to avoid disputes.

"The outside noise is gone, but now the 'whoosh' of the fan is getting picked up by my mic and it's unusable."
"When I turn on the AC, it makes a noise in a frequency range that noise canceling can't fully remove."

Despite investing heavily in a soundproof room, many streamers and creators suffer from <strong> "Indoor Equipment Noise." </strong>

Let's be direct. The solution for air flow noise in a soundproof room is not a simple filter cleaning. It's solely about <strong> "Lowering Wind Velocity" </strong> and <strong> "Controlling the Airflow Path." </strong>

In this article, we explain the correct answers for "Silent Tuning" inside a soundproof room from a professional studio designer's perspective.

## 1. Why do Fans and ACs Feel "Loud" in a Soundproof Room?

### Reason 1 : Lowered Background Noise (Ambient Noise)

Because a soundproof room cuts external noise to the extreme, the "Ambient Noise Level" (original silence) becomes extraordinarily low. Fan or AC noises that wouldn't bother you in a normal room stand out as "Roaring" in a Dr-35 to Dr-40 space.

### Reason 2 : Increased Wind Velocity due to "Small Space" and "High Airtightness"

To exchange air in a small, airtight space, strong force is needed to "Push in" or "Pull out" air. Trying to maintain required ventilation through a small opening inevitably increases the air speed (velocity), causing friction against the vent's slats (grilles) and generating high-frequency "hissing" or "rushing" wind noise.

## 2. Three Approaches to Eradicating Fan Noise

Technical approaches to fundamentally solve fan noise are as follows :

### 1. Expanding Ventilation Duct Diameter (Velocity Reduction)

Based on basic fluid dynamics, if you keep the airflow volume the same, <strong>doubling the duct diameter reduces the velocity to 1/4</strong>. When velocity drops, friction noise drops drastically.
For DIY rooms or renovations, simply expanding a vent from Φ100mm to Φ150mm becomes surprisingly quiet.

### 2. Implementing Silencers (Acoustic Ducts)

The use of a silencer (flexible acoustic duct) that "lets air through but blocks sound" is mandatory.
Use ducts with built-in glass wool and arrange them in an <strong> "S-shape" or "U-shape" (Sound Trap structure) </strong> rather than a straight line. This allows fan motor noise and airflow noise to be absorbed by the duct walls, preventing noise from entering the room.

### 3. The Ultimate Weapon : "Lossnay" (Heat Exchange Ventilation)

The most reliable investment is the "Lossnay" (by Mitsubishi Electric).
While normal fans just "poke a hole and pull air," a Lossnay exchanges air while maintaining room temperature and has high isolation performance (D-40 equivalent). Its silence on the "Low" setting is extremely high, making it "Standard Equipment" for condenser mic recording environments.

## 3. AC (Air Conditioning) Noise Suppression Strategy

AC is a lifeline to prevent heatstroke, but it is also a vicious noise source.

### Utilizing Mic "Directionality" in Placement

If using a cardioid mic, the "Rear" of the mic is the dead point (blind spot) where it picks up the least sound.
Always place the AC indoor unit <strong> "Behind the Mic" (e.g., behind your monitor) </strong>. If the AC is in front or directly above the mic, it will capture wind noise directly.

### The Absolute Rule : "Fixed Low Flow" During Recording

"Auto" mode is strictly prohibited. It will detect temperature changes and suddenly increase fan speed, ruining your take.
The professional protocol is to cool (or heat) the room on "High" 30 minutes before recording, then <strong>fix it to "Low" or "Silent Mode" right before recording/streaming</strong>, with the airflow set to a ceiling swing to avoid hitting the mic directly.

## 4. DON'T DO THIS! Dangerous Amateur Mistakes

Avoid these common internet "solutions" as they severely compromise safety and isolation :

- <strong>Streaming with the Fan Turned Off</strong> :
  If you stop ventilation in a room smaller than 1.5-tatami (approx. 2.3sqm), CO2 concentrations reach thousands of ppm in just 30–60 minutes, causing headaches, fatigue, or worse, loss of consciousness from oxygen deprivation. <strong> "Long-term use with ventilation off" is life-threatening. </strong>

- <strong>Stuffing Acoustic Foam into Vents</strong> :
  While it reduces wind noise, it blocks the air path, resulting in zero ventilation. This leads to the oxygen deprivation risk mentioned above and drastically lowers AC efficiency.

## Conclusion : Silence is Born from "Airflow Control"

No matter how thick your walls are, if you cannot control the "Airflow" of ventilation and AC, the quality of your streaming or recording will suffer.

1. <strong>Build a silent ventilation system using Lossnay, etc.</strong>
2. <strong>Attenuate noise with silencer ducts.</strong>
3. <strong>Strategically place the AC and use fixed low airflow based on mic directionality.</strong>

By ensuring these steps, your soundproof room finally functions as a "Complete Studio." Do not skimp on equipment investment—build a safe and noiseless environment.

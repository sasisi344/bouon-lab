## Vocal-Optimized Acoustic Material Selection

Choose acoustic materials optimized for singing covers:

### Acoustic Material Frequency Characteristics

For singing frequencies (80Hz–4000Hz), appropriate materials are essential.

<strong>Frequency Characteristics by Material</strong>

| Material | Mid-High | Bass | Overall Rating |
|---|---|---|---|
| <strong>Fiberglass Wool 100mm</strong> | ◎◎ | ◎ | ★★★★★ |
| <strong>Urethane Foam 100mm</strong> | ◎◎ | △ | ★★★★☆ |
| <strong>Rockwool 75mm</strong> | ◎ | ◎ | ★★★★★ |
| <strong>Tencel 50mm</strong> | ◎ | ◎ | ★★★☆☆ |

<strong>Recommended Choice for Singing Covers</strong>

### Fiberglass Wool 100mm (Optimal)

<strong>Why</strong>

- <strong>Completely covers vocal frequency range</strong>
- <strong>Excellent bass control</strong>
- <strong>Best cost-performance ratio</strong>

<strong>Placement Method</strong>

| Position | Thickness | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 100mm | Echo elimination (highest priority) |
| Ceiling | 50mm | Overhead echo elimination |
| Sides | 100mm | Side reflection elimination |
| Rear | 50mm | Background sound absorption |

<strong>Installation Procedure</strong>

1. Create wooden frame support structure
2. Pack fiberglass wool into frame
3. Cover with non-woven fabric (itch prevention)
4. Apply interior finish from front

### Urethane Foam 100mm (Alternative)

<strong>Advantages</strong>

- <strong>Easy handling</strong>
- <strong>Safety-focused</strong>
- <strong>Aesthetic appearance</strong>

<strong>Disadvantages</strong>

- <strong>Weaker bass absorption than fiberglass</strong>
- <strong>Limited durability (approx. 5 years)</strong>

## Practical Recording Techniques for Singing Covers

Recording techniques after vocal booth completion:

### Microphone Setup

<strong>Gain Setting</strong>

- <strong>Target:</strong> Peak meter at -6dB
- <strong>Test:</strong> Record at louder volume
- <strong>Adjust:</strong> Optimize within -3dB to -9dB range

<strong>Pop Filter Setup</strong>

- <strong>Essential accessory</strong>
- <strong>Position 5cm from microphone</strong>
- <strong>Eliminate plosive sounds ("p," "t")</strong>

### Recording Environment Preparation

<strong>Booth Interior Environment</strong>

| Item | Target |
|---|---|
| <strong>Temperature</strong> | Ideally 18–22°C |
| <strong>Humidity</strong> | 40–60% RH recommended |
| <strong>Lighting</strong> | LED (silent) |
| <strong>Other Equipment</strong> | External placement (avoid fan noise) |

### Recording Procedure

1. <strong>Warm-up</strong> (10 minutes)
- <strong>Warm vocal cords</strong>
- <strong>Final microphone position check</strong>

2. <strong>Test Recording</strong> (3 minutes)
- <strong>Test actual volume</strong>
- <strong>Adjust gain</strong>

3. <strong>Main Recording</strong> (5–10 minutes)
- <strong>Multiple takes (3–5 attempts)</strong>
- <strong>Select best take</strong>

4. <strong>Verification & Adjustment</strong>
- <strong>Playback confirmation</strong>
- <strong>Re-record if issues found</strong>

## Common Singing Cover Recording Issues & Solutions

Typical problems and solutions:

### Issue ①: Excessive Reverb

<strong>Cause</strong>

- <strong>Insufficient acoustic material</strong>
- <strong>Improper placement</strong>

<strong>Solution</strong>

- <strong>Add acoustic material (sides/ceiling)</strong>
- <strong>Position microphone closer to front panel</strong>

### Issue ②: Background Noise Present

<strong>Cause</strong>

- <strong>AC/PC fan noise</strong>
- <strong>External sound</strong>

<strong>Solution</strong>

- <strong>Relocate equipment to separate room</strong>
- <strong>Avoid recording during noisy times</strong>

### Issue ③: Voice Sounds Cold

<strong>Cause</strong>

- <strong>Excessive acoustic absorption</strong>
- <strong>Low microphone sensitivity</strong>

<strong>Solution</strong>

- <strong>Adjust absorption (reduce 100mm to 75mm)</strong>
- <strong>Increase microphone gain</strong>

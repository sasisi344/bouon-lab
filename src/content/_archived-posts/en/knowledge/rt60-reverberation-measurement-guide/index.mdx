Built a soundproof room but notice "too much echo" or "muddy sound"?

The cause is <strong>RT60 (reverberation time)</strong> not being optimized to ideal values.

RT60 is the critical metric for <strong>quantifying acoustic environment</strong> in soundproof rooms. This guide details RT60 measurement methods, optimal values, and improvement techniques.

## RT60 (Reverberation Time): Understanding the Basics

First, let's establish fundamental concepts:

### RT60 Definition

<strong>RT60</strong> = Time required for sound to decay 60dB

```
Sound source generated
  ↓
Maximum volume (0dB)
  ↓
60dB attenuation
  ↓
Measurement complete (time = RT60)
```

<strong>Examples</strong>

- <strong>RT60 = 0.3 seconds:</strong> Sound decays quickly (heavy absorption)
- <strong>RT60 = 0.5 seconds:</strong> Good balance (professional grade)
- <strong>RT60 = 1.0 seconds:</strong> Excessive echo (insufficient absorption)

### Why RT60 Matters

| Item | Short RT60 | Optimal RT60 | Long RT60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| <strong>Sound reflections</strong> | None | Natural | Excessive |
| <strong>Vocal quality</strong> | Cold | Warm | Muddy |
| <strong>Streaming/content</strong> | Favorable | Optimal | Unfavorable |
| <strong>Movie watching</strong> | ○ | ◎ | △ |

<strong>Optimal RT60 by Use</strong>

- <strong>Podcast/streaming:</strong> 0.2–0.4 seconds
- <strong>Vocal recording:</strong> 0.3–0.5 seconds
- <strong>Music production:</strong> 0.4–0.6 seconds
- <strong>Movie/gaming:</strong> 0.5–0.8 seconds

## RT60 Adjustment Methods

Post-measurement optimization:

### ① Too Long RT60 (Increase Absorption)

<strong>Strategies</strong>

| Strategy | Effect |
|---|---|
| <strong>Add absorption material</strong> | Reduce RT60 by 0.1–0.3 sec |
| <strong>Add carpeting</strong> | Primarily absorbs bass |
| <strong>Absorption curtains</strong> | Absorbs treble |
| <strong>Furniture placement</strong> | Reduces reflections |

<strong>Implementation Procedure</strong>

1. Measure current RT60
2. Calculate target gap
3. Estimate required absorption material
4. Add incrementally
5. Re-measure at each stage

<strong>Calculation Formula</strong>

```
Added material area = (RT60 difference / 0.1 sec) × Current material area

Example: Reduce 0.6 sec RT60 to 0.3 sec
(0.6 - 0.3) / 0.1 = 3× absorption needed
```

---

### ② Too Short RT60 (Reduce Absorption)

<strong>Strategies</strong>

| Strategy | Effect |
|---|---|
| <strong>Partially remove materials</strong> | Extend RT60 |
| <strong>Increase reflective surfaces</strong> | Especially extends bass |
| <strong>Add reverb</strong> | Electronic supplement |

<strong>Implementation Procedure</strong>

1. Partially remove absorption panels
2. Make rear walls reflective
3. Re-measure for verification

---

### ③ Frequency-Specific Differences

<strong>When Bass Too Long</strong>

- <strong>Add bass traps (corner treatment)</strong>
- <strong>Use large absorption materials</strong>

<strong>When Treble Too Long</strong>

- <strong>Add treble absorption (thin panels)</strong>

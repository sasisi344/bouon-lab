A soundproof room is not a "buy and forget" product. The high-level airtightness and isolation performance are guaranteed to <strong> "degrade" </strong> over time if neglected.

However, with proper maintenance, you can extend the typical 10–15 year lifespan expected by manufacturers to <strong> over 20 years. </strong>

In this article, we explain the <strong> "Periodic Inspection Protocol to Protect Asset Value" </strong> practiced by professionals.

## Conclusion : Lifespan is Determined by "Seals" and "Ventilation"

The physical lifespan of a soundproof room depends more on <strong> "moving parts" and "air passages" </strong> than on the wall panels themselves.

- <strong>Hardening of Door Gaskets (Seals)</strong> : Rubber seals lose elasticity due to dryness and aging. A mere 1mm gap can drop isolation performance from Dr-40 to Dr-30.
- <strong>Ventilation Efficiency Drop</strong> : Soundproof room fans have specialized silencing structures that easily accumulate dust. If airflow drops, CO2 levels rise, threatening both your gear's thermal health and your own focus.

## 15-Minute Periodic Inspection Protocol

Perform this 15-minute check once a month to catch early signs of failure.

- <strong>Airtightness Test (The Paper Test)</strong> : Place a sheet of A4 paper between the door and the frame and close the door. If the paper pulls out easily at any point, the seal needs replacement or the door hinges need adjustment.
- <strong>Fan Noise Check</strong> : Listen for high-pitched whirring or unusual vibrations from the ventilation unit. Bearing lifespan is shortened by mold and dust accumulation.
- <strong>Joint Tightness Verification</strong> : Vibrations from playing instruments can gradually loosen panel screws. Periodically check for light leaks at joints, which indicate physical gaps.

## Fighting the 3 Enemies : Mold, Dust, and Air Sealing Failure

### Mold : The Result of High Airtightness

Soundproof rooms trap humidity. In summer, ensure a dehumidifier is running to keep humidity below 60%. If mold appears, use <strong> rubbing alcohol </strong> immediately to eradicate it before it penetrates deep into the absorbers.

### Dust : The Enemy of Acoustic Treatment

Fabric or fiberglass absorbers attract dust. If left alone, this changes the "acoustic tuning" (causing muffled highs). Weekly cleaning is as important as tuning an instrument.

### Seal Failure : A Critical Performance Bottleneck

Soundproofing performance is only as strong as its weakest point. Ignoring a single cable-hole gap or a peeling seal is equivalent to abandoning the precision machinery that is your soundproof room.

## Protecting Asset Value : Brand Cycles vs. DIY Limits

Branded units like <strong> Yamaha (Avitecs) or Kawai (Nasal) </strong> retain high value in the second-hand market if maintenance records are kept.

- <strong>DIY Tasks</strong> : Filter cleaning, hinge lubrication, minor seal repairs, LED replacements.
- <strong>Professional Tasks</strong> : Internal AC cleaning, severe mold remediation, major hinge replacements, and re-measuring acoustic performance.

## Summary : Routine Care Preserves the Sanctuary

Maintenance is not just cleaning—it is Asset Management.

1. <strong>Ventilate for at least 5 minutes</strong> after every use session.
2. <strong>Check seal elasticity once a month</strong> to maintain airtightness.
3. <strong>Clean filters biannually</strong> to ensure quiet, clean airflow.

This simple routine will keep your expensive investment as a "Sanctuary of Silence" for 20 years and beyond.

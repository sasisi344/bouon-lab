## Overview

When thunderstorms and typhoons strike, thunder noise can instantly create fear and anxiety indoors. For those interested in soundproofing, the burning questions are: "What level of sound isolation performance is needed to mitigate thunder noise?" and "What structures and equipment are most effective?" This article organizes thunder noise countermeasures from a soundproofing specialist perspective.

## Thunder Noise and Sound Pressure Levels

Let's first understand the magnitude of thunder noise accurately.

<strong>Sound Pressure Levels by Thunder Type</strong>

- Direct lightning strike at close range: <strong>100-120dB</strong> (jet engine, concert level)
- Thunder from several hundred meters to kilometers away: <strong>70-90dB</strong> (subway car, loud voice level)

<strong>Important Point</strong>: <strong>Complete elimination is impossible</strong>, but "raising sound isolation performance to convert it to distant thunder" is entirely achievable.

## Sound Isolation Performance by Building Structure

The basic structure of a building directly affects soundproofing performance.

| Structure | Sound Isolation Performance (external wall target) | How Thunder Sounds |
|---|---|---|
| Wooden construction (traditional method) | 25-35dB | Resonates almost as-is |
| 2×4 wooden construction | 30-40dB | Slightly mitigated but explosive sound remains |
| Lightweight steel frame | 30-40dB | Equivalent to wood, low frequencies pass through easily |
| RC construction (reinforced concrete) | 45-55dB | Reduced to distant thunder level |
| RC construction + dual windows | 50-60dB | Significantly reduced, fear diminishes |

<strong>Reading Tip</strong>: Whether in a wooden house or RC apartment, <strong>window reinforcement alone is insufficient</strong> for adequate soundproofing effects.

## Window Soundproofing is Critical

Thunder noise <strong>enters primarily through windows rather than walls</strong>. Window treatment is the top priority.

### Dual Windows (Inner Sash)

<strong>Performance</strong>: +10-15dB improvement
<strong>Cost</strong>: ¥50,000-150,000 per window

The air gap created between the installed inner window and existing sash dramatically improves sound isolation performance.

<strong>Advantages</strong>
- Installable in rental housing
- Most products allow restoration to original state

<strong>Disadvantages</strong>
- Installation costs if replacement is needed

### Soundproof Glass

<strong>Performance</strong>: +10-20dB improvement
<strong>Cost</strong>: ¥100,000-200,000 per window

Uses thick laminated or dual-pane glass. Requires replacing existing glass, making it suitable for owner-occupied properties.

### Soundproof Curtains

<strong>Performance</strong>: Several dB improvement (psychological reassurance effect)
<strong>Cost</strong>: ¥10,000-30,000 per window

Weak alone, but mitigates reflected sound and provides psychological comfort.

### Storm Shutters & Soundproof Shutters

<strong>Standard Storm Shutters</strong>
- <strong>Performance</strong>: Approximately 5-6dB reduction
- <strong>Cost</strong>: Several thousand yen
- <strong>Features</strong>: Easy improvement

<strong>Soundproof Shutters</strong>
- <strong>Performance</strong>: Up to 30dB reduction
- <strong>Cost (manual)</strong>: ¥60,000-160,000
- <strong>Cost (electric)</strong>: ¥100,000-350,000
- <strong>Features</strong>: Makes thunder "very distant." Blocks both sound and light

## Room-Level Soundproofing

Converting one room to soundproof specifications creates a "refuge room" against thunder noise.

| Construction Method | Sound Isolation Performance | Estimated Cost | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sound isolation sheet + double drywall | ~50dB | ¥200,000-500,000 per room | Most practical construction |
| Simple soundproof booth (ASMR/instrument) | 40-50dB | ¥200,000-800,000 per booth | Modular, movable. Thunder remains but fear diminishes |
| Studio-grade soundproof room | 60dB+ | ¥1,000,000-3,000,000 per room | Professional construction, reduces thunder to distant level |

### Construction Method Details

<strong>Sound Isolation Sheet + Double Drywall Method</strong>
1. Apply sound isolation sheet to existing wall
2. Attach double layer of drywall
3. Place sound-absorbing material inside

Advantages: Guaranteed effectiveness, relatively low cost
Disadvantages: Construction period, noise during work

## High-Performance Housing Options

If searching for new housing, consider these selection criteria.

### Structure Selection Guidelines

<strong>Wooden Houses</strong>
- Window and wall reinforcement essential
- Professional soundproofing may be necessary

<strong>RC Apartments</strong>
- Standard 45dB+ sound isolation performance
- Window reinforcement alone can be sufficient

<strong>Single-Story vs. Multi-Story</strong>
- Single-story: Stronger ground reflection
- Multi-story first floor: Weaker direct impact

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

<strong>Q: I live in a wooden house. Where should I start with thunder countermeasures?</strong>
A: Window reinforcement is the first priority. Start with dual window installation or soundproof shutters.

<strong>Q: I'm in rental housing where construction isn't possible. What options exist?</strong>
A: Consider soundproof curtains, noise-canceling earphones, and white noise generators—all reversible solutions.

<strong>Q: Are soundproof room costs prohibitive? Are there alternatives?</strong>
A: Simple soundproof booths (¥200,000-800,000) or closet conversion (¥100,000-200,000) are effective alternatives.

<strong>Q: Is there an optimal season for thunder countermeasures?</strong>
A: The measures themselves are season-independent, but completing installation before rainy season (May-July) or autumn rain season (September-October) provides peace of mind.

## Summary

Thunder is a natural phenomenon we cannot stop. However, by combining soundproofing techniques, you can transform "explosive thunder" into "distant thunder" and create a home that protects concentration and sleep.

### Priority Order for Countermeasures

1. <strong>Top Priority</strong>: Window reinforcement (dual windows, soundproof shutters)
2. <strong>Secondary</strong>: Room-level soundproofing (single dedicated quiet space)
3. <strong>Long-term</strong>: Building structure selection (when house hunting)

### For Optimal Selection

- For work-from-home and sleep protection, <strong>RC construction + dual windows</strong> is ideal
- Balancing cost and performance, aim for <strong>approximately 50dB sound isolation</strong> as a realistic target

Start today with measures suited to your situation. Soundproofing is a valuable investment that delivers long-term comfort.

In a single-room apartment, the biggest threat to a streamer's survival is the <strong> "Neighbor Complaint (Wall-Slam)" </strong>.

Investing in a professional soundproof unit costing over $10,000 is unrealistic for most aspiring streamers and students.

However, if you correctly understand the <strong> "Physical Mechanism of Sound Leakage," </strong> it is possible to drastically lower complaint risks even with a limited budget of under $100.

This article casts aside the "occult soundproofing" myths like "just stick sponges on the wall" and provides <strong> "Knowledge-based Soundproof Hacks" </strong> based on studio design principles.

## 1. Core Principle : Kill the "Gaps" Before Thickening Walls

Sound is a vibration of air. No matter how many expensive foam panels you line up on the wall, if your door undercut or vents are open, acoustic energy will leak out <strong> "like water" </strong>. Your first investment should be "Gaps," not "Walls."

- <strong>Total Use of Gap Tape</strong> (Budget : ~$10) : Completely seal the gaps between the door frame and the floor with thick rubber tape. This alone improves sound isolation by several decibels—a level you can clearly feel.
- <strong>Soundproof Sleeves for Vents</strong> (Budget : ~$20) : Insert dedicated soundproof sleeves (silencers) into the wall vents. This prevents outside noise from entering and stops your late-night talk from being broadcast to the outside.

## 2. Device Isolation | Reducing the Physical Energy of Your Voice

If you cannot upgrade the soundproofing of the room itself, the most logical approach is to reduce the <strong> "Total Amount of Sound Generated" </strong>.

- <strong>Leverage the "Proximity Effect"</strong> : Use a mic arm to place the microphone within 5 cm of your mouth. This allows you to pick up even whispers with rich, clear bass, physically eliminating the <strong> "Need to Shout" </strong>.
- <strong>Hardware-linked Noise Removal</strong> : Use AI noise removal like NVIDIA Broadcast to erase keyboard clicks and AC noise. This allows you to avoid unnecessarily raising your mic gain (sensitivity), focusing purely on voice capture.

## 3. Redefining Interior Items as "Absorbers & Barriers"

Before buying expensive dedicated panels, calculate the mass and absorption of the daily items already in your one-room apartment.

- <strong>Position the Bed as a "Target" (Acoustic Trap)</strong> : If possible, place your bed or a thick sofa in the direction you speak (behind the monitor or against the facing wall). Mattresses and blankets act as <strong> "Giant Fabric Traps" </strong> that efficiently absorb low-to-mid voice energy.
- <strong>Heavy Blackout Curtains (with Lining)</strong> : Windows are the weakest points in a room due to their lack of mass. Ensure your curtains are long enough to drag on the floor and cover the entire window. This dampens glass resonance and high-frequency leakage.

## Conclusion : One-Room Soundproofing is "Defense," Not a "Wall"

With a $100 budget in a one-room apartment, aiming for perfect silence (Dr-40 level) that stops all sound to the next room is impossible. The goal is nothing more than <strong> "Keeping leaked sound within the neighbor's tolerance (acceptable stress line)" </strong>.

Thoroughly seal physical gaps, suppress voice output with mic work, and absorb resonance with furniture. By practicing this triple-threat "Hack," you will be freed from the fear of "Wall-Slams" and can focus on more relaxed and creative streaming.

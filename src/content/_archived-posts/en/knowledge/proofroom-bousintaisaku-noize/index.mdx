"Installed soundproof room but got complaints from downstairs..." Ever experienced this?

Actually, even with excellent soundproof room sound insulation, floor vibration and low-frequency noise are separate issues. Especially for drums or heavy bass, standard soundproofing alone is insufficient. This article explains floor vibration and low-frequency noise characteristics through specific vibration isolation countermeasures.

## Why Floor Vibration and Low-Frequency Noise Are Problematic

First, understand the difference between "sound insulation" and "vibration isolation."

### Airborne Sound vs. Structure-Borne Sound

Sound transmission has two main types:

<strong>Airborne Sound:</strong>
- Transmission: Through air
- Examples: Conversation, singing, high-pitched instruments
- Standard soundproof room handling: Effective
- Performance indicator: D-value

<strong>Structure-Borne Sound (Vibration):</strong>
- Transmission: Through floors/walls/building structure
- Examples: Footsteps, drums, bass low frequencies
- Standard soundproof room handling: Insufficient
- Performance indicator: L-value (impact sound)

<strong>Key Point:</strong> D-values don't indicate vibration isolation performance.

### Why Soundproof Rooms Don't Stop Vibration

<strong>Soundproof Room Design:</strong>
- Walls: Excellent airborne sound blocking
- Doors: Sealed against air sound transmission
- Purpose: Prevent airborne sound leakage

<strong>However:</strong>
- Floors: Usually direct contact with building
- Impact transmission: Through structural connections
- Low frequencies: Wavelengths too long for standard absorption

<strong>Result:</strong> Even D-70 soundproof rooms barely reduce floor vibration.

## DIY Vibration Reduction

### Layered Approach

<strong>Floor Layers (Bottom to Top):</strong>
1. Building floor (existing)
2. Rubber mat (10mm)
3. Cork board (5mm)
4. Plywood (12mm)
5. Carpet with padding

<strong>Total Cost:</strong> 50,000-120,000 yen
<strong>Expected Reduction:</strong> 20-28dB
<strong>Installation:</strong> DIY possible with care

### Installation Tips

<strong>For Piano:</strong>
- Platform directly under piano only (not whole room)
- Ensure level
- Secure placement
- Furniture sliders for mobility

<strong>For Drums:</strong>
- Riser platform essential (whole kit)
- Double-layer isolation ideal
- Kick drum: Additional measures
- Consider electronic kit alternative

## Low-Frequency Specific Countermeasures

### Understanding Low Frequencies

<strong>Characteristics:</strong>
- Wavelength: Several meters long
- Propagation: Through entire building
- Blocking: Most difficult
- Common sources: Bass, subwoofers, kick drums

### Bass Management

<strong>Frequency Reduction:</strong>
- Don't over-emphasize bass in practice
- Use headphones for bass-heavy work
- Strategic EQ: Reduce excessive low-end
- Volume moderation: Especially low frequencies

<strong>Isolation:</strong>
- Floating floors most effective
- Thick rubber: Helps but limited
- Active cancellation: Emerging technology

## Measurement and Verification

### Vibration Testing

<strong>Simple Test:</strong>
1. Place water glass on floor below
2. Play instrument/create impact above
3. Observe water ripples
4. Assess vibration transmission

<strong>Interpretation:</strong>
- Significant ripples: High vibration
- Slight ripples: Moderate vibration
- No ripples: Low vibration

<strong>Professional Measurement:</strong>
- Vibration meter: 40,000-120,000 yen
- Professional service: 60,000-200,000 yen
- Quantified results guide investment

- Below typical life sounds
- Neighbor tolerance range

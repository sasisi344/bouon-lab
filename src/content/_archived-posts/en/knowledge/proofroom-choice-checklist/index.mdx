Don't want to buy a soundproof room only to regret "This isn't what I expected..." Actually, soundproof room selection has completely different priority points depending on use and environment.

This article organizes pre-selection checkpoints by use case. Even first-time soundproof room shoppers will understand how to choose appropriately.

## Soundproof Room Selection | 3 Key Decisions First

To avoid soundproof room selection failures, determine these before browsing catalogs.

### 1. Clarify Purpose

"Instrument practice," "streaming," "remote work"—necessary soundproofing varies significantly by purpose.

<strong>Required Performance (D-Value) by Instrument:</strong>
- <strong>Conversation/singing:</strong> D-40~50
- <strong>Piano:</strong> D-50~55
- <strong>Wind instruments:</strong> D-50~55
- <strong>Drums:</strong> D-65~70
- <strong>Streaming/DTM:</strong> D-45~60

Lower-frequency instruments like drums require higher soundproofing. Casual "just want a soundproof room" selection invites later "Sound still leaks..." regrets.

### 2. Verify Installation Location Conditions

<strong>Essential Checks:</strong>
- <strong>Available space (exterior dimensions, not just interior)</strong>
- <strong>Ceiling height</strong>
- <strong>Floor load capacity</strong>
- <strong>Access route (doors, stairs, elevators)</strong>
- <strong>Building permissions (if rental)</strong>

<strong>Space Requirements:</strong>
- <strong>1.5 tatami room interior needs ~2.5-3 tatami total space</strong>
- <strong>2 tatami room needs ~3.5-4 tatami space</strong>

### 3. Set Realistic Budget

Including installation and accessories:
- <strong>Simple booth:</strong> 100,000-300,000 yen
- <strong>Standard unit:</strong> 800,000-1,500,000 yen
- <strong>Premium:</strong> 1,500,000-3,000,000+ yen
- <strong>Custom renovation:</strong> 3,000,000-10,000,000+ yen

<strong>Total Budget Components:</strong>
- <strong>Unit cost</strong>
- <strong>Delivery/installation</strong>
- <strong>Floor protection</strong>
- <strong>Accessories</strong>
- <strong>Annual maintenance</strong>

## Use-Case Specific Checklists

### For Piano Players

<strong>Priority Ranking:</strong>

1. Size (★★★★★)
- <strong>[ ] Interior accommodates piano + bench + movement</strong>
- <strong>[ ] 1.5-2 tatami minimum for upright</strong>
- <strong>[ ] 2.5+ tatami for grand pianos</strong>

<strong>2. Sound Insulation (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-50~55 minimum</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-55~60 preferred for apartments</strong>

<strong>3. Acoustics (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Interior doesn't sound too dead</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Pleasant practice environment</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Adjustable acoustic treatment</strong>

<strong>4. Ventilation (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Adequate air circulation</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Climate control compatible</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Long session comfort</strong>

<strong>5. Floor Strength (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Combined weight (piano + room) verified OK</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Reinforcement if needed</strong>

### For Wind Instrument Players

<strong>Priority Ranking:</strong>

1. Sound Insulation (★★★★★)
- <strong>[ ] D-50~60 for brass</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-45~55 for woodwinds</strong>

<strong>2. Interior Acoustics (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Not too dry (some natural reverberation desirable)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Comfortable sound reflection</strong>

<strong>3. Size (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] 1-1.5 tatami usually adequate</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Standing room comfortable</strong>

<strong>4. Humidity Control (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Ventilation adequate</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Moisture management</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Instrument protection</strong>

### For Drums

<strong>Priority Ranking:</strong>

1. Vibration Isolation (★★★★★)
- <strong>[ ] Floating floor or equivalent</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Ground floor preferred</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Dedicated vibration measures</strong>

<strong>2. Sound Insulation (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-65~70 minimum</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-70+ ideal</strong>

<strong>3. Size (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] 2+ tatami for full kit</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Height adequate for cymbal stands</strong>

<strong>4. Structural Strength (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Heavy equipment support</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Shock absorption</strong>

<strong>Alternative:</strong> Electronic drums significantly reduce requirements

### For Streaming/Content Creation

<strong>Priority Ranking:</strong>

1. Size vs. Comfort (★★★★★)
- <strong>[ ] Desk + chair + equipment fits</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Extended session comfort</strong>
- <strong>[ ] 1.5-2 tatami preferred</strong>

<strong>2. Acoustics (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Minimal echo</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Clear voice recording</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Acoustic treatment included or budget for</strong>

<strong>3. Sound Insulation (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-45~55 adequate for most</strong>
- <strong>[ ] D-55~60 for singing/ASMR</strong>

<strong>4. Climate Control (★★★★★)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Adequate for equipment heat</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Long-session viable</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Ventilation sufficient</strong>

<strong>5. Power Supply (★★★★☆)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Adequate outlets (4-6 minimum)</strong>
- <strong>[ ] Circuit capacity sufficient</strong>

## Common Selection Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Choosing Too Small

<strong>Problem:</strong> Buyer's remorse, discomfort, limited use
<strong>Solution:</strong> When in doubt, size up (better resale value too)

### Mistake 2: Insufficient D-Value

<strong>Problem:</strong> Still receives complaints
<strong>Solution:</strong> Add 5-10dB safety margin to minimum requirements

### Mistake 3: Ignoring Ventilation

<strong>Problem:</strong> Unusable in summer
<strong>Solution:</strong> Verify climate control from start

### Mistake 4: Overlooking Total Cost

<strong>Problem:</strong> Budget exhausted, can't afford installation/accessories
<strong>Solution:</strong> Budget comprehensively 120% of unit cost

## Decision Framework

### Must-Have vs. Nice-to-Have

<strong>Must-Haves:</strong>
- <strong>Adequate D-value for use</strong>
- <strong>Sufficient size for comfort</strong>
- <strong>Affordable total cost</strong>
- <strong>Proper installation feasibility</strong>

<strong>Nice-to-Haves:</strong>
- <strong>Premium finish</strong>
- <strong>Advanced features</strong>
- <strong>Perfect aesthetics</strong>
- <strong>Maximum performance</strong>

<strong>Focus on must-haves first; nice-to-haves only if budget allows.</strong>

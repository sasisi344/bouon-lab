The moment you close the soundproof room door, you enter a <strong> "Micro-Airtight Space" </strong> completely isolated from the outside world.

This airtightness is essential for soundproofing, but it is a double-edged sword for climate control.

If you run a humidifier with the same mindset as a normal living room, the back of your acoustic foam will become a breeding ground for mold in hours. Conversely, if left neglected, the wood of expensive instruments will scream and crack.

In this article, we teach you the <strong> Physically Correct Humidity Strategy </strong> for soundproof rooms.

## Conclusion : Airtight Spaces Have an "Extremely Small Humidity Buffer"

Soundproof rooms (especially 1-2 tatami unit types) have an extremely small volume compared to standard rooms, and air exchange is restricted.

- <strong>Rapid Saturation</strong> : Human breath contains moisture. Just practicing for one hour in a small room can raise relative humidity by over 10%.
- <strong>Lack of Buffer</strong> : Because walls are covered with isolation panels, you cannot expect the "Humidity Buffering" function (absorbing and releasing moisture) found in wooden houses. All moisture stays in the space, causing the environment to collapse quickly if neglected.

## The Ultrasonic Trap : Why "White Dust" Erodes Gear and Instruments

Ultrasonic humidifiers, often chosen for streaming booths due to their silence, carry a fatal risk.

- <strong>Mineral Dispersion (White Dust)</strong> : Since ultrasonic units turn water into particles and spray them, they disperse calcium and magnesium from tap water into the space. When dry, this becomes <strong> "White Dust" </strong>, which adheres to microphone diaphragms (reducing sensitivity), clogs PC fans, and wears down piano mechanisms.
- <strong>Solution</strong> : In a soundproof room, the iron rule is to choose <strong> "Evaporative" </strong> (filtering) or <strong> "Steam-Heated" </strong> (which doesn't disperse impurities). If you must use an ultrasonic unit, use only purified water.

## Blind Spot of Condensation : The Horror of "Dew Point" Behind Walls

In winter humidification, the most dangerous thing is not visible condensation on windows, but <strong> "Behind the Walls." </strong>

- <strong>Calculating Dew Point</strong> : As humidity rises, the dew point (the temperature where condensation starts) also rises. If the outside of the soundproof wall faces a cold hallway, the inner surface temperature of the panel may drop below the dew point, causing condensation between the absorber and the panel.
- <strong>Mold Sanctuary</strong> : Once moisture is absorbed by materials like glass wool or polyester, it is hard to dry, and mold breeds quickly. These spores not only harm the performer's health but also emit odors throughout the room.

## Optimal Humidity by Instrument : Managing VPD (Vapor Pressure Deficit)

To protect instruments, you need the awareness of keeping "Drying Pressure (VPD)" constant rather than just a湿度 percentage.

- <strong>Woodwinds & Strings (50% ± 5%)</strong> : To avoid "Dryness Shock" the moment you take the instrument out of its case, keeping the room environment constant year-round defines the instrument's lifespan.
- <strong>Piano (45% – 55%)</strong> : To prevent key sticking and pitch instability, management based on 24-hour humidity logging is recommended.

## Summary : Monitor the Trend of "Absolute Humidity," Not just the Meter

Steps to maintain a comfortable environment in a soundproof room :

1. <strong>Install digital smart hygrometers that keep records in several locations.</strong>
2. <strong>Select "Evaporative" or "Steam" humidifiers to prevent white dust.</strong>
3. <strong>In summer, use the AC's "Reheat Dehumidification" to maintain 50% humidity.</strong>

A soundproof room must be an <strong> "Environmental Control Unit" </strong> to protect delicate instruments and gear, not just a "Sound Shelter." Wise climate management based on physical phenomena supports the sustainability of your musical life.

Are you struggling with loud aircraft noise because you live near an airport? In Japan, there is a <strong>residential soundproofing subsidy system</strong> where the government or airport companies cover the cost of insulation work in designated high-noise areas.

If your home is eligible, you can replace windows and doors with high-performance soundproof models with virtually no out-of-pocket cost. This article provides a clear guide on how to check eligibility and the application process in Japan.

## What is the Airport Soundproofing Grant? Public Support for Noise Mitigation

The grant for soundproofing projects around airports is based on laws such as the <strong>"Act on Prevention, etc. of Damage Caused by Aircraft Noise in the Vicinity of Public Aerodromes."</strong>

- <strong>Purpose</strong> : To reduce noise pollution from aircraft takeoffs and landings and improve living conditions.
- <strong>Funding Sources</strong> : Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism (MLIT/Airport Companies) or the Ministry of Defense (for Self-Defense Forces bases).
- <strong>How it Works</strong> : It covers the installation of soundproof windows, doors, and air conditioning systems for homes in noise-affected areas.

Since this system is a compensation for external noise factors, it often covers <strong>100% of the construction cost</strong> if criteria are met.

## How to Check Eligibility for "Type I Noise Zone" Area Standards

Not all houses near an airport are eligible. Your home must be located within a designated <strong>"Noise Zone" (Type I Zone, etc.)</strong>.

- <strong>Measurement Grade (Lden)</strong> : Designated based on a noise index (formerly WECPNL). Zones starting from <strong>62 Lden</strong> or higher are typically eligible.
- <strong>Housing Age Requirements</strong> : The house must have existed at the time the airport noise zone was officially designated. Homes built _after_ the designation are often ineligible.
- <strong>Verification Method</strong> : You can check the <strong>"Noise Maps"</strong> published on the websites of airport operators or local defense bureaus of the Ministry of Defense.

Don't assume your house is eligible just because it's noisy; verify the exact boundary on official maps first.

## What is Covered by the Grant? 100% Subsidy Conditions

The grant-funded renovation essentially aims to "block noise while ensuring ventilation."

1.  <strong>Soundproof Sashes and Doors</strong> : Replacement with high-performance double-glazed sashes or thick soundproof doors.
2.  <strong>Ceiling and Wall Insulation</strong> : Adding sound-absorbing materials inside the wall structure.
3.  <strong>Air Conditioning Units (AC/Vent Fans)</strong> : Since you need to keep windows closed to block noise, the cost of essential ventilation and heating/cooling equipment is bundled.

Notably, <strong>replacement of AC units after 10 years</strong> of use may also be eligible for further subsidies. Continuous maintenance support is a significant benefit.

## Application and Construction Process | Schedule and Required Documents

While individuals can apply, it is smoother to work with a <strong>"Registered Contractor"</strong> who has experience in government-funded soundproofing.

- <strong>1. Eligibility Check</strong> : Confirm your house is in the designated zone at the local office or defense bureau.
- <strong>2. Site Survey</strong> : A contractor visits to survey noise leakage and identify required work areas.
- <strong>3. Grant Application</strong> : Prepare documents (Resident Card, Property Registration, etc.) and submit them for approval.
- <strong>4. Construction</strong> : Once approved (Grant Decision), the actual renovation begins.
- <strong>5. Inspection and Payment</strong> : After completion, a final inspection is done, and the grant is paid out.

Expect the entire process from application to completion to take <strong>about 6 months to 1 year</strong>.

## Summary: Utilize Airport Grants to Improve Your Living Environment

If you live in an eligible airport noise zone in Japan, this grant is a highly beneficial system for home improvement.

- Check if your home is in the <strong>Noise Zone (Type I, etc.)</strong>.
- Verify if the <strong>house age and structure</strong> meet the criteria.
- Consult with a <strong>trusted registered contractor</strong> specialized in soundproofing.

If you suspect your home might be eligible, contact the nearest airport operator or local defense bureau. It's the first step toward regaining peace and quiet in your daily life.

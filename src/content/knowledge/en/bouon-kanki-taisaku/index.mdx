In soundproof room design, <strong> "Ventilation" and "Sound Isolation" are in a physically opposing trade-off. </strong>

To exchange air, you need holes (openings) in the wall, but those holes are the easiest escape routes for sound. However, in a sealed space of about 1-tatami (1.5sqm), the CO2 concentration from an adult male will rise to levels that impair focus in just 15 minutes.

In this article, we explain the <strong> "Silenced Ventilation System" and the reality of Lossnay </strong> required to balance silence and oxygen.

## Conclusion : Vents are "Sound Escape Routes" and Carry Full Responsibility

No matter how high the wall's D-value is, if the ventilation treatment is weak, all sound will leak through. The basic strategy to prevent this is "Sound Attenuation."

- <strong>Chamber Structure</strong> : By making the ventilation path zigzag and lining the interior with absorbers, you attenuate sound energy against the walls while letting air through (maze structure).
- <strong>Duct Silencing</strong> : You must stop both fan noise and internal sound leakage simultaneously. In cheap booths, the "Air Intake/Exhaust" is where most leakage occurs.

## The Magic of Lossnay : Oxygen Without Sound Leakage

The <strong> "Lossnay" (by Mitsubishi Electric) </strong>, standard in major rooms like Yamaha or Kawai, is a life-support system for soundproof rooms.

- <strong>Merits of Heat Exchange</strong> : It swaps cold winter air or hot summer air with the heat of the exhausting air. This provides fresh oxygen while preventing rapid room temperature changes.
- <strong>Isolation via Barriers</strong> : Inside a Lossnay, air and sound are managed through a special paper element (Lossnay Element), blocking a direct "Air Path." This is the physical basis for its high sound isolation.

## The Challenge of NC Values : Silent Ventilation Defines Recording Quality

For recording engineers, what matters is not just isolation, but <strong> "How quiet the room is (NC Value)." </strong>

- <strong>Reducing Airflow Noise</strong> : If the fan speed is too high, a "whooshing" wind noise occurs at the vent, which condenser mics will pick up.
- <strong>Improving S/N Ratio</strong> : A quiet ventilation system ensures a high Signal-to-Noise (S/N) ratio, allowing you to capture minute details. When selecting a Lossnay, choose a size that provides sufficient ventilation even at "Low" speed.

## AC Installation Pitfalls : Preventing Leakage via Pipes

AC is also mandatory for heat, but there is a trap here as well.

- <strong>Pipe Sleeve Gaps</strong> : Fill the hole for refrigerant pipes with an excessive amount of "Soundproof Putty." A tiny gap will cause D-50 performance to crumble.
- <strong>Drain Hose Backflow Noise</strong> : External noise (traffic) can enter through the drainage hose. Installing a check valve is an essential detail for a professional environment.

## Conclusion : A Soundproof Room is Complete Only with Silence and Oxygen

Environmental measures are as important as isolation performance—they define "Sustainable Use."

1. <strong>Design based on the premise of "Being able to breathe"</strong> (Lossnay recommended).
2. <strong>Always install a silencer chamber on vents</strong> (block the direct path of sound).
3. <strong>Ensure absolute airtightness of AC pipe sleeves.</strong>

What you need for your best performance is not just silence, but a <strong> "Cool, oxygen-rich, and quiet space." </strong> When these three elements align, your soundproof room becomes a true creative sanctuary.

Are you hesitating to buy a professional soundproof booth like "Danbotchi" because of its 100,000 yen+ price tag? In Japan, you can build a similar performance booth for <strong>about 50,000 yen</strong> by combining materials easily found at local hardware stores.

In this article, I reveal the blueprints and material selection tips for a simple DIY soundproof booth I personally designed.

## Why Build Your Own? Advantages and Budget for DIY Booths

The biggest advantage of DIY is achieving a <strong>"perfect fit for your room"</strong> at an <strong>"overwhelmingly low cost."</strong>

- <strong>Cost Savings :</strong> Aim for the same sound insulation performance (-20dB) with less than half the budget.
- <strong>Customizability :</strong> Easily mount monitor arms or add extra ventilation fans.
- <strong>Satisfaction :</strong> The feeling of streaming or practicing in a space you built yourself is exceptional.

Total material costs are typically <strong>between 45,000 and 55,000 yen</strong> when using timber frames and plastic cardboard (Pladan) with sound-insulating sheets.

## [Blueprints Revealed] Material List for a 1-Tatami Size Soundproof Booth

Here are the key points of the design, based on standard-sized timber for easy building.

- <strong>Frame :</strong> Use 30mm square cedar timber or 2x4 (Two-by-Four) lumber.
- <strong>Wall Material :</strong> Plastic cardboard (Pladan) with a thickness of 4mm or more.
- <strong>Mass (Insulation) :</strong> Sound-insulating sheets (e.g., Daiken Soundproof Sheet 455H).
- <strong>Absorption (Interior) :</strong> White Kyuon (polyester fiber) or acoustic foam.

The basic structure is a <strong>3-layer sandwich</strong> : Timber frame -> Pladan or Plywood outside -> Sound-insulating sheet and absorption material inside.

## Choosing Materials: Pladan, Timber, and Sound-Insulating Sheets

In material selection, never compromise on <strong>"mass"</strong> and <strong>"thickness."</strong> Soundproofing performance is directly proportional to material density.

- <strong>Pladan :</strong> Do not pick thin sheets for floor protection. Choose <strong>"heavy-duty hollow structure" sheets.</strong>
- <strong>Timber :</strong> Select dry timber with minimal warping and take full advantage of the hardware store's cutting service.
- <strong>Sound-Insulating Sheets :</strong> While online stores offer more variety, when buying locally, pick the <strong>"heavy and dense" rolls.</strong>

Choosing thin sheets just because they are cheap will result in failing to block low-frequency sounds.

## Step-by-Step Assembly | Strengthening Frames and Sealing Gaps

The most critical factor in assembly is eliminating <strong>"gaps"</strong> where sound leaks.

1.  <strong>Frame Assembly</strong> : Secure timber with screws and reinforce corners with L-brackets.
2.  <strong>Wall Attachment</strong> : Fasten Pladan tightly using a staple gun (tacker).
3.  <strong>Use Sealing Tape</strong> : Always apply rubber weatherstripping tape to the door edges.
4.  <strong>Caulking</strong> : Filling joints with silicone caulk alone can improve insulation by 3-5dB.

Think of it as building a watertight box. Check from the inside to see if any light leaks through; if light gets through, sound will too.

When the noise from a neighbor becomes unbearable, or if you are a streamer worried about your own volume, the word <strong>"Police"</strong> often comes to mind. 

But can the police actually stop the noise? And for streamers, how can you prevent a police visit from destroying your career?

This article explains the reality of police intervention in noise disputes and how to protect yourself.

## 1. What the Police Can Actually Do

In most residential areas, noise is a civil matter. However, the police can and will respond to noise complaints.

- <strong>Warning and Guidance:</strong> Officers will visit the source of the noise and give a verbal warning. This is often effective for temporary noise (like a loud party).
- <strong>Mediation:</strong> They act as a neutral third party to prevent the situation from escalating into a physical altercation.
- <strong>Reporting to Landlords:</strong> In some cases, a police record of the visit can be used as evidence for a property management company to take action.

<strong>What they CAN'T do:</strong>
- <strong>They generally cannot make an arrest or issue a fine for standard "neighbor noise" (unless it's a criminal disturbance of the peace).</strong>
- <strong>they cannot force someone to install soundproofing.</strong>

## 2. The Risk for Streamers and VTubers

For those who broadcast from home, a police visit is more than just an inconvenience; it can be a "leak" of your real-world identity or a traumatic event for your audience.

- <strong>Accidental Complaints:</strong> Neighbors may hear shouting and assume a crime is occurring (e.g., domestic violence) when you are simply excited during a game.
- <strong>Malicious 'Swatting':</strong> Unfortunately, malicious viewers may call the police to your address.

## 3. Prevention: The Power of Preemptive Greetings

The best way to deal with the police is to ensure they never have a reason to visit.

- <strong>Pre-Stream Greeting:</strong> A simple "I will be doing some online broadcasting/voice recording in the evenings; please let me know if it's ever too loud" goes a long way.
- <strong>Testing with Neighbors:</strong> Ask a neighbor to listen while you speak at your normal streaming volume to find the limit.

## Conclusion

The police are a last resort for noise victims and a major risk for noise creators. By combining physical soundproofing with <strong>social soundproofing</strong> (good neighbor relations), you can create an environment where the police are never needed.

## 1. Noise Fragments the "Sleep Cycle"

What determines sleep quality is <strong>the depth and duration of "non-REM sleep" (deep sleep).</strong>
Studies show that external noise (even at 40dB, the level of a quiet library) can pull deep sleep back into "shallow sleep," even without waking you up.

With a soundproof room (Dr-35+), it's possible to suppress nighttime outside noise almost to the point of <strong>"complete silence (20dB or less)."</strong> Thus, the brain is convinced of <strong>"100% safety"</strong> and enters an unprecedentedly deep relaxation mode.

Many users practicing ultra-quiet sleep report <strong>"feeling alert even with shorter sleep durations."</strong>

-  <strong>Conventional bedroom</strong>: Spend 8 hours sleeping, but the brain repeatedly "micro-wakes" dozens of times due to noise.
-  <strong>Inside a soundproof room</strong>: Spend 6 hours sleeping, but without a single interruption to the cycles, ensuring smooth excretion of brain waste products (Amyloid-β, etc.).

## 3. 3 Things to Watch Out For When Sleeping in a Soundproof Room

However, because soundproof rooms aren't designed as "bedrooms," some measures are necessary.

1.  <strong>Optimizing Ventilation Systems</strong>: Dual ventilation fans are mandatory. An increase in CO2 concentration in a sealed space will actually shallow sleep and cause a headache upon waking.
2.  <strong>Removal of Electromagnetic Waves and Static Electricity</strong>: Do not overcrowd the small space with electronic devices; being aware of earthing (static removal) will yield a deeper relaxation effect.
3.  <strong>Adapting to "Complete Silence"</strong>: Sometimes, extreme silence makes the sound of your own heartbeat or blood flow noticeable. In that case, playing a very small amount of white or pink noise can help you enter a more natural sleep state.

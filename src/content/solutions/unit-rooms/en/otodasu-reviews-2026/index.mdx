## 1. The Physics Gap: Why Pro Booths are Heavy

Brands like Yamaha or Kawai build booths to be "100% soundproof" on their own. This requires lead-lined panels and concrete-grade density. 

- <strong>The Problem</strong>: These booths weigh between 800 lbs and 2,000 lbs. They requires floor reinforcement and specialized movers at $2,000+ per relocation. For a renter, this is a non-starter.

## 2. The Hybrid Solution: OTODASU (The 70 lb Framework)

In Japan, the <strong>OTODASU</strong> shell has revolutionized the market. Made from lightweight plastic-corrugate (Pladan), it weighs only <strong>70 lbs (30kg).</strong>

- <strong>The Strategy</strong>: Don't treat OTODASU as the final soundproof wall. Treat it as a <strong>"Precision-Sealed Air Container."</strong> 
- <strong>The Cost</strong>: Starting at ~$800 USD, it provides the "Room-in-Room" framework that is 100% reversible and movable by one person.

## 3. The "Engineer's Layer" Hack

To turn a $1,000 lightweight booth into a $10,000-grade environment, you apply three specific Tokyo-standard layers:

1.  <strong>Acoustic Foam Inside</strong>: Line the interior with 2-inch (50mm) acoustic wedge foam. This kills the "boxiness" of the recording and creates a studio-grade internal response.
2.  <strong>Exterior MLV (Mass Loaded Vinyl)</strong>: Drape the outside of the booth with a high-density acoustic sheet (like *Sandam*). This adds the "Mass" required to block voices and high-frequency noise without the permanent structure of a pro booth.
3.  <strong>The "Floating Floor"</strong>: Place the booth on top of the <strong>Shizuyuka + P-Mat</strong> layering system. This decouples the booth from the building structure, stopping vibration from your PC fans or your movements from reaching the microphone.

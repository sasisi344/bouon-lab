"I'm hesitant about choosing a soundproof room size" "I don't want to regret it being too narrow after buying it"

For those with such concerns. <strong>Choosing a soundproof room size is not just a matter of 'spaciousness', but is determined by the balance of three factors: purpose, budget, and installation space.</strong>

In this article, we provide a 'map of size choice' covering the characteristics of each major size and criteria for success.

---

## Which is the Correct Answer? Three Success Criteria for Size Choice

First, let's diagnose which 'spaciousness' you should aim for with three points.

### 1. Choose by Instrument/Purpose
*   <strong>0.8 to 1 Tatami</strong>: Video streaming, telework, vocals, violin.
*   <strong>1.5 Tatami</strong>: Upright piano, saxophone, flute, cello.
*   <strong>2 Tatami or more</strong>: Grand piano, multiple people, DTM, relaxed practice.

### 2. Choose by the Size of the Room to Install
Soundproof rooms have 'external dimensions'. Even if the interior is 1.5 tatami, including the thickness of the walls, it occupies <strong>about 3 tatami mats worth</strong> of the room.
*   <strong>6-tatami room</strong>: 1.5 tatami is the limit (living space will be almost gone).
*   <strong>8-tatami room or more</strong>: 2 tatami or more can also be considered.

### 3. Choose by Budget
*   <strong>Up to $3,000</strong>: Simple soundproof room (around 0.8 tatami).
*   <strong>Around $10,000</strong>: Standard for unit soundproof rooms (1.5 tatami class).
*   <strong>$15,000 and more</strong>: Large/high-performance models (2 tatami and more).

## Detailed Guide for Each Size

Let's look closer at the size that fits your purpose.

### 1. [Extremely Small] 0.8 to 1 Tatami: For Streaming/Single Person Use
A size for those who want to "keep it cheap" and "space-saving".

### 2. [Standard] 1.5 Tatami: The Most Popular Golden Size
The minimum size that an upright piano fits into, and chosen by the most instrumentalists. If you're lost, this size is the basic.

### 3. [Spacious] 2 Tatami or More: Focus on Grand Piano/Comfort
A size for professional/semi-pro levels who want to "enjoy the original resonance of the instrument" or "want a space where they won't get tired even after a long time".

## Final Checklist Before Purchase

Lastly, we have summarized the items you must check before signing a contract.

1.  <strong>Check Installation Space (External Dimensions)</strong>: Is it the 'external dimensions' from the catalog that fit in the room, not the interior dimensions?
2.  <strong>Measure Carry-in Route</strong>: Have you measured the width of the entrance, hallway, elevator, and doors?
3.  <strong>Floor Load Capacity</strong>: Is it okay to place a nearly 1-ton soundproof room on the 2nd floor of a wooden building? (Must check with the management company or construction company)
4.  <strong>Air Conditioning (AC)</strong>: Is your heatstroke countermeasure for summer solid?
5.  <strong>Comparison with Used/Rental</strong>: Is buying a new one really the best choice?

## Summary: Find Your 'Correct Answer'

A soundproof room is a once-in-a-lifetime big purchase. Once installed, the size cannot be changed (other than replacement).

1.  <strong>For mainly streaming or telework</strong> → <strong>0.8 to 1 Tatami</strong>
2.  <strong>For upright piano or wind/string instruments</strong> → <strong>1.5 Tatami</strong>
3.  <strong>For grand pianos or seeking the best resonance</strong> → <strong>2 Tatami or more</strong>

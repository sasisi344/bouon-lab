Even without expensive panels, you can improve your room's soundproofing by simply <strong>moving your furniture.</strong>

When setting up a home office or bedroom, most people focus on "neatness." However, if you focus on <strong>"Acoustic Zoning,"</strong> you can create a much more peaceful environment.

## 1. The "Window Buffer" Rule

Windows are the weakest point for sound.
- <strong>Don't place your bed or desk directly under a window if you live on a noisy street.</strong>
- <strong>Do move your bed to the wall furthest from the window and use heavy furniture (like a bookshelf full of books) as a "mass buffer" against the wall shared with a neighbor.</strong>

## 2. Desk Orientation for Streamers/Meetings

- <strong>Facing the Wall:</strong> If you sit with your back to the room and face the wall, your voice hits the wall directly and "bounces" back into the microphone, often leaking to the next room.
- <strong>The 'Island' Layout:</strong> If possible, sit facing into the room. Use the air space of the room to dissipate your voice before it hits the walls.

## 3. The Power of "Soft Surfaces"

Sound reflects off hard, flat surfaces (floors, ceilings, desks).
- <strong>Rugs:</strong> A thick rug under your chair stops "floor impact noise" (moving your chair).
- <strong>Curtains as Walls:</strong> If one wall is particularly thin, hanging a decorative "wall curtain" can dampen echoes and make the room feel much quietier.

## Conclusion

Your room layout is your "Zero-Cost Soundproofing." By understanding how sound moves through your specific apartment, you can strategically place your desk and bed to shield yourself from the most intrusive noises.

This document provides a detailed database of technical specifications for various soundproofing products, from complete room units to individual components. It is designed to be a permanent reference for content creation.

## 1. Understanding Soundproof Performance Ratings

### 1.1. Key Metrics

- **Dr-Value / D-Value:** A Japanese Industrial Standards (JIS) rating for sound insulation. The higher the number, the better the performance. It measures how much sound is blocked across different frequencies.
  - **Dr-30:** Reduces loud speech to a murmur. Instrument practice at night is difficult.
  - **Dr-35:** Reduces a piano to the level of a low conversation. Suitable for daytime use.
  - **Dr-40:** Reduces a piano to a whisper. Night practice becomes feasible.
- **Transmission Loss (TL / dB):** Measures the reduction in sound pressure level (in decibels) as sound passes through a material. A -25dB reduction means the sound level is significantly lowered.
- **STC (Sound Transmission Class):** The North American standard, similar to Dr-Value.

### 1.2. Airborne vs. Structure-Borne Sound

- **Airborne Sound:** Travels through the air (e.g., voices, TV, musical instruments). Blocked by mass and airtightness. This is what Dr/D/STC ratings primarily measure.
- **Structure-Borne Sound (Vibration):** Travels through solid materials (e.g., footsteps, piano pedals, drum kicks). Requires **vibration isolation** (e.g., floating floors, damping mats), not just standard soundproofing.

## 3. Key Components & Materials

### 3.1. Windows & Doors

- **Inner Windows (Double-Glazing):** The most effective solution for window soundproofing. Creates a large air gap.
  - **Top Manufacturers:** LIXIL (Inplus), YKK AP (Plamade U), Taishin Kogyo (Plast - favored by audiophiles for extreme airtightness).
  - **Glass Types:**
    - **Laminated Glass:** Two glass panes bonded with a sound-damping film. Excellent for sound but poor for insulation.
    - **Dissimilar-Thickness Double Glazing:** Uses two panes of different thicknesses (e.g., 5mm + 3mm) to prevent resonance. **The best choice for balancing soundproofing and insulation (and for subsidies).**
    - **Vacuum Glass (Spacia):** The ultimate in both soundproofing and insulation, but very expensive.
- **Soundproof Doors:** High-mass, airtight doors with multiple seals are critical. A standard hollow-core door is a major weak point.

### 3.2. Vibration & Acoustic Treatment

- **Vibration Damping Mats:** Essential for structure-borne noise.
  - **Concept:** A combination of a soft layer (to absorb) and a heavy rubber layer (to block) is key.
  - **Recommended Products:** P-Bouon Mat (high-density rubber), Shizuka Yuka Light (sound-absorbing carpet tile). The combination of these two is a powerful solution.
- **Acoustic Panels:** Control reflections *inside* the room for better sound quality. Made from materials like Glass Wool or Rock Wool. They absorb sound, preventing echo and flutter.
- **Sound Insulating Sheets:** High-density vinyl or rubber sheets added inside walls to increase mass and block airborne sound.

### 3.3. Essential Accessories

- **Soundproof Outlet/Switch Boxes:** Standard electrical boxes are holes in the wall. Airtight, sealed boxes (e.g., from Mirai Kogyo) are a must to prevent sound leakage.
- **Ventilation:** Airtight rooms require mechanical ventilation to prevent oxygen depletion and heat buildup. However, the fan itself can be a noise source, so quiet models or duct silencers are necessary for recording.
- **Spot Coolers (External Setups):** For small booths (OTODASU/Danbotchi) where internal AC is impossible. Use an external unit and feed the cold air through a specialized "Silencer Duct" to maintain isolation. Recommended for mid-range streamers.
- **Pass-through LAN (Cat 6A/7):** To maintain low-latency gaming/streaming without relying on Wi-Fi (which is blocked by metal/aluminum shielding). Uses flat-cables or sealed wall-ports to prevent sound leakage.
- **High-Efficiency Ventilation Fans:** For lightweight booths where thermal buildup is rapid. Use fans with sound traps (baffles) to prevent noise leakage.
- **LED Lighting:** Choose flicker-free, low-heat, and silent (no buzzing) LED lights. Panel lights are often preferred for their even, soft illumination, which is ideal for video streaming.

## 4. Instrument-Specific Requirements

| Instrument Category | Recommended Dr-Rating | Min. Space (Tatami) | Key Soundproofing Challenge & Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Piano (Upright/Grand)** | **Dr-35 to Dr-40+** | 1.7+ (UP) / 3.0+ (GP) | **Structure-Borne Vibration:** Heavy weight and pedal action require floor reinforcement and robust vibration damping mats. |
| **Drums (Acoustic/Electronic)** | **Dr-60+** (Acoustic) / **Dr-35+** (Electronic) | 3.0+ | **Extreme Vibration:** Kick drum and pedal impacts are the #1 complaint source. A "floating floor" is often necessary. Standard units are insufficient for acoustic drums. |
| **Brass & Woodwinds** | **Dr-35 to Dr-40** | 1.2+ | **Heat & Air Quality:** High airtightness + heavy breathing = rapid heat/CO2 buildup. AC and proper ventilation are mandatory. |
| **Strings (Violin/Cello)** | **Dr-30 to Dr-35** | 1.2+ | **Humidity & Space:** Instruments are sensitive to humidity. Requires enough room for bow movement without hitting walls. |
| **Vocals & Streaming** | **Dr-35 to Dr-40** | 0.8+ | **Noise from Equipment:** Ventilation fan noise is a critical issue for high-quality recording. Must be managed. |

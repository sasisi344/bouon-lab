# Soundproof Room Implementation Guide

Soundproof room investment requires careful planning to prevent noise complaints, heat issues, and high removal costs.

### 1. Performance & Design

- **Specs vs. Reality:** Catalog Dr-values (sound insulation) are lab-tested. Actual performance drops in-room due to ventilation, door gaps, and existing wall quality.
- **Solid-borne Sound:** Piano and drums vibrate floors/walls. "Floating floor" structures are mandatory for vibration-heavy instruments; standard units only block air-borne sound.
- **Target Setting:** Clear goals are needed (e.g., night-time piano requires Dr-40+ to reduce noise to "whisper" levels in adjacent rooms).

### 2. Living Environment & Safety

- **Mandatory Air Conditioning:** High airtightness leads to risk of heatstroke. Pre-confirm wall drilling and piping access before installation.
- **Fire Safety:** Fire alarms are legally required in soundproof units. Multi-family buildings may require fire-safety inspections and professional installation.
- **Floor Load:** Professional units weigh hundreds of kilograms. Wooden or old structures may require floor reinforcement.

### 3. Apartments & Rentals

- **"Instrument-Friendly" vs. "Soundproof":** Most "musical instrument allowed" rentals lack soundproofing. Complaints can lead to eviction or performance bans.
- **Management Approval:** Always obtain written consent from management or owners before installing units.
- **Logistics:** Confirm assembly space and delivery routes (elevator, stairs, hallway width).

### 4. Costs & Subsidies

- **Subsidy Limitations:** Government subsidies rarely apply to hobby-based soundproofing (piano/theater), unless combined with specific energy-efficiency window renovations.
- **Disposal Costs:** Units are specialized waste. Professional removal costs ¥10k to ¥100k+, though high-quality units have resale value.

### 5. DIY Limitations

- **Insulation vs. Absorption:** Foam panels (DIY) only stop echoes, not leakage. True soundproofing requires "mass" and "sealing." Poor DIY risks floor damage or oxygen deprivation from bad ventilation.

### Implementation Checklist

1. **Goal:** Instrument type, volume, and practice hours?
2. **Impact:** Are vibration measures sufficient for neighbors?
3. **Comfort:** AC and ventilation confirmed?
4. **Logistics:** Management permission and delivery route secured?
5. **Exit:** Plan for future removal, relocation, or resale?
